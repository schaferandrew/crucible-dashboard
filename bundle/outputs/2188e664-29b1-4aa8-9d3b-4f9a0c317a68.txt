**Explanations**

- **a) The Emancipation Proclamation taking effect this New Year’s Day** – Issued Jan 1 1863; by June 10 it had already taken effect, so the writer could know about it.  
- **b) The siege of Vicksburg dragging on** – The siege was ongoing in 1863 and would end July 4; news of its continuation was possible.  
- **c) News of Lee’s defeat at Gettysburg** – The battle ended July 3, so on June 10 the defeat had not yet occurred; the writer could not have known this news.  
- **d) The telegraph bringing news from Washington** – Telegraph service was well‑established by 1863; receiving messages from Washington was feasible.  
- **e) The Monitor’s fight with the Merrimack last year** – The Battle of Hampton Roads occurred March 9 1862, long before June 10, so it could be known.  
- **f) A cousin in Cleveland who telephoned with the news** – The telephone was not invented until 1876; a phone call in 1863 is impossible.  
- **g) Fencing the north pasture with barbed wire** – Barbed wire was patented in 1874, far after 1863; fencing with it could not have happened then.  
- **h) Mathew Brady’s photographs of the dead at Antietam** – Antietam took place September 17 1862 and its photos were already in circulation by mid‑1863.  
- **i) Stonewall Jackson’s death last month** – He died December 19 1863, which is “last month” relative to June 10; the writer could have heard of it.  
- **j) West Virginia, which joined the Union last month** – West Virginia had already declared independence in May and was admitted as a state by August; news of its union status would be known.  
- **k) The draft riots in New York** – The riots occurred July 19 1863, after June 10, so the writer could not have known they had happened.

**ANACHRONISMS:** c, f, g, k
