Port Ardmore City Council approved a $41.6 million transit levy that funds three new bus routes and rebuilds the Fulton Street station; council member Ines Varga voted no because the city had not published a cost estimate for households, Mayor Theo Brandt called it “the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation,” and Rob Haddad’s opinion column argues the levy will cost a typical household $900 per year and that the council rushed the vote [S1, S2, S9 opinion]

On Tuesday morning a fire broke out early at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore; initial reports said two firefighters were injured, but later an update corrected that only one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released while crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m.; the cause remains under investigation [S3, S4]

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance held a rally outside City Hall on Monday evening; Ledger reporters estimated the crowd at roughly 300, whereas organizers reported that more than 1,000 people attended and police gave an estimate of about 400 [S5, S6]

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500 by receiving 39‑28 Senate votes and 81‑52 House votes; Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week according to a spokesperson [S7]

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, marking its third increase this year and citing inflation at 4.1% [S8]
