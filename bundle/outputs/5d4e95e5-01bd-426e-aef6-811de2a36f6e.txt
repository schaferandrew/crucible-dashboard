The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years, which will fund three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station; council member Ines Varga, who voted no, said the city had not published a cost estimate for households, while Mayor Theo Brandt hailed it as the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation and said collection would begin July 1 if the levy passes its second reading on March 24. Columnist Rob Haddad argues the levy will cost a typical household $900 a year and calls it a tax grab writing that 'Nobody asked the people paying for it' [S1, S2, S9 opinion]

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore; an initial report from Statewide Wire said two firefighters had been injured at the scene, but Fire Chief Ama Mensah later clarified that only one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released, and that crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m.; the cause remains under investigation [S3, S4]

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening; Ledger reporters at the scene estimated the crowd at about 300 however organizers of the rally said more than 1,000 people attended while police put the estimate at 400 [S5, S6]

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500 by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week a spokesperson said and the increase would take effect in the fall term [S7]

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, its third increase this year, citing inflation of 4.1% [S8]
