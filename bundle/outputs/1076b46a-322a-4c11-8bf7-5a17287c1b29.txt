## Problem 1

Count integers in [1, 10000] divisible by 3 or 7 (union), then remove those also divisible by 21.

- Divisible by 3: ⌊10000/3⌋ = 3333
- Divisible by 7: ⌊10000/7⌋ = 1428
- Divisible by both (i.e., by 21): ⌊10000/21⌋ = 476

Union: $3333 + 1428 - 476 = 4285$. Every multiple of 21 lies in this union, so subtract all 476:
$$4285 - 476 = 3809.$$

## Problem 2

Powers of 7 mod 100 cycle with period 4: $7,\,49,\,43,\,1$ (since $7^4 = 2401 \equiv 1$).
$$2026 \bmod 4 = 2 \implies 7^{2026} \equiv 7^2 = 49.$$

## Problem 3

Let $f(n)$ count tilings of a 2×n board. Classify by the tile covering the top-right cell:
- **Vertical domino** (fills column n): leaves f(n−1)
- **Two horizontal dominoes** into column n−1: fills columns n and n−1, leaves f(n−2)
- **2×2 square**: fills columns n and n−1, leaves f(n−2)

$$f(n) = f(n-1) + 2f(n-2), \qquad f(0)=1,\ f(1)=1.$$

Check: $f(2)=3$ (two verticals, two horizontals, one square ✓), $f(3)=5$ ✓.

$$\begin{array}{c|c}

0&1\\1&1\\2&3\\3&5\\4&11\\5&21\\6&43\\7&85\\8&171\\9&341\\10&683\\11&1365\\12&\mathbf{2731}
\end{array}$$

## Problem 4

Largest is exactly 4 ⟺ all dice ≤ 4, but not all ≤ 3:
$$P = \left(\tfrac{4}{6}\right)^3 - \left(\tfrac{3}{6}\right)^3 = \frac{8}{27} - \frac{1}{8} = \frac{64-27}{216} = \frac{37}{216}.$$

## Problem 5

Each child has $2\times 7 = 14$ equally likely types; sample space is $14^2 = 196$.

Let B = "at least one boy-Tuesday." Children who are *not* boy-Tuesday: girls (7) + non-Tuesday boys (6) = 13.
$$|B| = 196 - 13^2 = 27.$$

Both-boys with at least one Tuesday-boy: both-boys outcomes $= 7\times7 = 49$, minus those with no Tuesday $= 6\times 6 = 36$:
$$|A\cap B| = 49 - 36 = 13.$$

$$P(\text{both boys}\mid B) = \frac{13}{27}.$$
