Here are six geography questions designed to challenge common assumptions:

1. Which country is crossed by both the Equator and the Tropic of Capricorn?
   *Justification: Many assume Brazil, but it's actually Ecuador that lies between these two lines.*

2. Which country has coastline on both the Red Sea and the Persian Gulf?
   *Justification: Saudi Arabia is often overlooked in favor of Egypt or Oman, but it borders both bodies of water.*

3. What is the northernmost capital city of a sovereign state?
   *Justification: Reykjavik (Iceland) is frequently mistaken for the answer, but it's actually Longyearbyen (Svalbard), though not a capital—so the correct answer is Reykjavik after all.*

4. Counting overseas territories, which country spans the most time zones?
   *Justification: France is often underrated compared to Russia or the US, but its territories cover 12 time zones.*

5. Which US state is closest to mainland Africa?
   *Justification: Maine seems like a logical choice, but it's actually Florida due to the proximity of the Canary Islands.*

6. Apart from the microstates inside Italy, which is the only landlocked country that borders exactly one other country?
   *Justification: Lesotho (surrounded by South Africa) is often forgotten in favor of Vatican City or San Marino.*

---

### ANSWERS
1. Ecuador
2. Saudi Arabia
3. Reykjavik (Iceland)
4. France
5. Florida
6. Lesotho
