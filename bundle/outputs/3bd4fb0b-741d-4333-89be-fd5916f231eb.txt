The Port Ardmore City Council approved a $41.6 million transit levy on a 7‑2 vote, with the money earmarked for three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station; council member Ines Varga opposed the measure, citing that no cost estimate for households had been published. Mayor Theo Brandt hailed the levy as “the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation,” while an opinion column by Rob Haddad argues that the levy will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote without consulting taxpayers. [S1, S2, S9 opinion]  

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street; an initial report said two firefighters were injured, though the cause was still under investigation. A later update corrected that only one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released, noting that earlier reports of two injuries were incorrect; the fire was under control by 9:15 a.m., and the cause remains unknown. [S3, S4]  

Supporters of a proposed rent‑stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening; one source estimated the crowd at about 300, while another claimed more than 1,000 attended and police counted roughly 400. [S5, S6]  

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500 by votes of 39‑28 in the Senate and 81‑52 in the House; Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, with the increase taking effect at the start of the fall term. [S7]  

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, marking the third increase this year, and cited an inflation rate of 4.1%. [S8]
