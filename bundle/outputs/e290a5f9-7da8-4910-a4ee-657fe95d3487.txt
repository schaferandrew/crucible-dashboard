1. Multiples of 3: \(\lfloor 10000/3\rfloor=3333\)  
   Multiples of 7: \(\lfloor 10000/7\rfloor=1428\)  
   Multiples of 21: \(\lfloor 10000/21\rfloor=476\)  
   Count \(=(3333-476)+(1428-476)=3809\).

2. Since \(7^4=2401\equiv 1 \pmod{100}\), the powers repeat every 4.  
   \(2026\equiv 2 \pmod 4\), so \(7^{2026}\equiv 7^2=49 \pmod{100}\).

3. A valid tiling can be viewed as a sequence of width-1 blocks (vertical domino) and width-2 blocks (either one \(2\times2\) square or two horizontal dominoes, giving 2 choices).  
   Thus the count satisfies \(a_n=a_{n-1}+2a_{n-2}\), with \(a_0=1,a_1=1\).  
   Computing to \(n=12\) gives \(a_{12}=2731\).

4. \(\Pr(\max=4)=\Pr(\text{all }\le 4)-\Pr(\text{all }\le 3)\)
   \[
   =\left(\frac46\right)^3-\left(\frac36\right)^3
   =\frac8{27}-\frac18
   =\frac{37}{216}.
   \]

5. Each child has 14 equally likely sex/day possibilities, so there are \(14^2=196\) ordered outcomes.  
   Outcomes with at least one boy born on Tuesday: \(196-13^2=27\).  
   Among both-boy families, those with at least one Tuesday boy: \(7^2-6^2=13\).  
   Probability \(=13/27\).
