The Port Ardmore City Council approved a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years to fund three new bus routes and rebuild the Fulton Street station, though council member Ines Varga said the city had not published a cost estimate for households and the levy still needs a second reading on March 24; Mayor Theo Brandt called it “the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation,” with collection beginning July 1 if it passes its second reading, but columnist Rob Haddad argues the levy is a tax grab that will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote. [S1, S2, S9 opinion]  

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street; an initial report said two firefighters were injured but Fire Chief Ama Mensah said one was treated for smoke inhalation and released earlier reports that two were injured were incorrect, with crews having the fire under control by 9:15 a.m. and the cause remaining under investigation. [S3, S4]  

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening; Ledger reporters estimated the crowd at about 300 while police estimate it was 400, and organizers said more than 1,000 people attended. [S5, S6]  

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500 with Senate votes of 39‑28 and House votes of 81‑52; Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, with the increase taking effect in the fall term. [S7]  

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, its third increase this year, citing inflation of 4.1%. [S8]  

Delphine Okoro won the open seat on the Port Ardmore school board with 52% of the vote. [S10]
