The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years, with the money will fund three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station, while council member Ines Varga, who voted no, said the city had not published a cost estimate for households, and the levy still needs a second reading on March 24; the council backed the transit levy on a 7-2 vote, Mayor Theo Brandt called it "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation," and collection would begin July 1 if the levy passes its second reading, while the opinion column in The Ardmore Bugle argued that columnist Rob Haddad argues that the transit levy will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote, with Haddad writing, "Nobody asked the people paying for it." A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore, and according to an initial report from the scene, two firefighters were injured, though Fire Chief Ama Mensah said one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released and that earlier reports that two firefighters were injured were incorrect, crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m., and the cause remains under investigation. The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House, and Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, a spokesperson said, and the increase would take effect in the fall term. [S7]

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening, and Ledger reporters at the scene estimated the crowd at about 300, while organizers of the Monday rally said more than 1,000 people attended and police estimated the crowd at 400. [S5, S6]
