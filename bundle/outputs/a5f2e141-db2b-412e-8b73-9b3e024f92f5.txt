The Port Ardmore City Council approved a $41.6 million transit levy over ten years on a 7-2 vote Monday to fund three new bus routes and rebuild the Fulton Street station, with collection set to begin July 1 if it passes its second reading on March 24 [S1, S2]. Mayor Theo Brandt called it "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation," while Council member Ines Varga opposed the measure, citing the lack of published household cost estimates. An opinion column by Rob Haddad in *The Ardmore Bugle* argued the levy amounts to a tax grab and criticized the rushed vote [S9 opinion]. 

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street, initially reported as injuring two firefighters, though Fire Chief Ama Mensah later corrected that one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released, with crews controlling the blaze by 9:15 a.m. [S3, S4]. The cause remains under investigation.

A rally supporting a proposed rent stabilization ordinance drew conflicting crowd estimates outside City Hall on Monday evening, with *Ardmore Ledger* reporters estimating about 300 attendees, while organizers claimed over 1,000 and police estimated 400 [S5, S6].

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, sending it to Governor Lena Ruiz for signature this week, with the increase set to take effect in the fall term [S7]. Separately, Veloria’s central bank raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, citing 4.1% inflation [S8].

Delphine Okoro won the open Port Ardmore school board seat with 52% of the vote in results announced March 3 [S10].
