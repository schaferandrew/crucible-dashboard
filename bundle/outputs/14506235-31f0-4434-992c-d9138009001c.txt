**a)** The Emancipation Proclamation was issued on January 1, 1863 and became effective that day. By June 10 the order was already in force, so the farmer could have known about it. **Not anachronism.**

**b)** The Siege of Vicksburg began May 18, 1863 and lasted until July 4. On June 10 it was still underway; news of the siege would have been circulating. **Not anachronism.**

**c)** The Battle of Gettysburg occurred July 1‑3, 1863. On June 10 the battle had not yet taken place, so Lee’s defeat could not have been known. **Anachronism.**

**d)** Telegraph lines connected Washington, D.C., to major cities by 1863; news could be transmitted instantly. The farmer might have received Washington‑area information via telegraph. **Not anachronism.**

**e)** The Monitor–Merrimack (Virginia) engagement was fought March 8‑9, 1862. By June 10, 1863, the event was a well‑known “last year” occurrence. **Not anachronism.**

**f)** The telephone was patented by Alexander Graham Bell in 1876; it did not exist in 1863. A cousin “telephoning” would have been impossible. **Anachronism.**

**g)** Barbed wire was invented by Joseph Glidden in 1874 and not used for fencing until the late 1870s. The farmer could not have fenced with it in 1863. **Anachronism.**

**h)** Mathew Brady photographed the dead at Antietam (September 17, 1862). By June 10, 1863 those images were already published and known. **Not anachronism.**

**i)** Stonewall Jackson died May 10, 1863; “last month” relative to June 10 refers to May. The farmer could have known of his death. **Not anachronism.**

**j)** West Virginia was admitted to the Union on June 20, 1863. It had not joined “last month” (May). The farmer could not have known that West Virginia had entered the Union a month earlier. **Anachronism.**

**k)** The New York Draft Riots occurred July 13‑16, 1863. On June 10 they had not yet happened; the farmer could not have known of them. **Anachronism.**

**ANACHRONISMS:** C,F,G,J,K
