# Analysis of Each Item (Letter dated June 10, 1863)

**a) Emancipation Proclamation taking effect "this New Year's Day"** — ANACHRONISM. The proclamation took effect January 1, **1863**, so by June it had already been in force for ~5 months. On a June letter, "this New Year's Day" would mean Jan 1, 1864, which is wrong.

**b) Siege of Vicksburg dragging on** — OK. The siege ran May 18 – July 4, **1863**, so it was very much ongoing on June 10.

**c) News of Lee's defeat at Gettysburg** — ANACHRONISM. Gettysburg ended July 3, **1863**, about three weeks *after* the letter's date.

**d) The telegraph bringing news from Washington** — OK. The telegraph was in widespread use throughout the Civil War.

**e) The Monitor's fight with the Merrimack last year** — OK. The Battle of Hampton Roads occurred March 8–9, **1862**, so "last year" (from June 1863) is correct.

**f) A cousin who telephoned with the news** — ANACHRONISM. The telephone was invented by Bell in **1876**, more than a decade later.

**g) Fencing the pasture with barbed wire** — ANACHRONISM. Barbed wire wasn't patented until **1874**.

**h) Mathew Brady's photographs of the dead at Antietam** — OK. Antietam was fought September 17, **1862**, and Brady's images circulated in late 1862/early 1863.

**i) Stonewall Jackson's death last month** — OK. Jackson died May 10, **1863**, so "last month" (May) is correct.

**j) West Virginia, which joined the Union last month** — ANACHRONISM. West Virginia was admitted June 20, **1863** — ten days *after* this letter's date.

**k) The draft riots in New York** — ANACHRONISM. The Draft Riots occurred July 13–16, **1863**, after the letter's date.

ANACHRONISMS: a, c, f, g, j, k
