The Port Ardmore City Council approved a $41.6 million transit levy over ten years by a 7-2 vote late Monday, with the funds designated for three new bus routes and the reconstruction of the Fulton Street station [S1]. Mayor Theo Brandt described the measure as "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation," noting that tax collection would begin on July 1 provided the levy passes its second reading on March 24 [S2]. Council member Ines Varga, who voted against the measure, stated that the city had not published a cost estimate for households [S1]. In an accompanying opinion piece, columnist Rob Haddad argued that the levy constitutes a "tax grab" that will cost a typical household $900 annually and criticized the council for rushing the vote without asking the taxpayers [S9 opinion].

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street, resulting in injuries to firefighters that were initially reported as two but later corrected by Fire Chief Ama Mensah to one person treated for smoke inhalation and released [S3, S4]. Crews had the blaze under control by 9:15 a.m., and the cause remains under investigation [S4].

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening, with conflicting estimates regarding the size of the crowd [S5, S6]. Ledger reporters at the scene estimated the attendance at about 300, while organizers claimed more than 1,000 people attended and police estimated 400 [S5, S6].

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, with final votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House [S7]. A spokesperson for Governor Lena Ruiz indicated she is expected to sign the bill this week, with the increase set to take effect in the fall term [S7].

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, marking its third increase this year and citing inflation of 4.1% [S8].
